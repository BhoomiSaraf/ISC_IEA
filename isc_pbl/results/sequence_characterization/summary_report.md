### Section 1 — Experiment Objective

The primary objective of this investigation is to evaluate whether the continuous, real-valued state sequences generated by the proposed **2D Ikeda–Sine–Chebyshev (ISC) chaotic map** can be converted into statistically uniform byte streams for an image-encryption pipeline.

In nonlinear dynamics, chaotic behavior in real-valued floating-point orbits is frequently confused with cryptographic security. However, high Lyapunov exponents or Kolmogorov–Sinai entropy in continuous phase space do not inherently guarantee that quantised byte sequences possess uniform distributions, low autocorrelation, or resistance to cryptanalysis. This research performs an empirical, reproducible benchmark between two principal quantisation paradigms:
1. **Fixed-Point Scaled-Floor-Modulo Transformation (Candidate Digit Extraction):** $B_n = \lfloor |x_n| \times 10^k \rfloor \bmod 256$ evaluated systematically across scaling exponents $k \in \{13, 14, 15, 16\}$.
2. **Arccos-based Probability Integral Transformation:** $u_n = \arccos(x_n)/\pi$, $B_n = \min(255, \lfloor 256 u_n \rfloor)$ derived from the theoretical arcsine invariant density of Chebyshev dynamics.

The goal is to determine empirical uniformity, precision constraints, and trade-offs to guide Step 2 of the ISC image cipher pipeline.

### Section 4 — Distribution Analysis & Controlled Ablations

The marginal probability distributions of $x_n$ and $y_n$ exhibit U-shaped profiles with boundary concentrations near $\pm 1$.
1. **Arcsine Invariant Measure Comparison:** In an idealized 1D Chebyshev map $x_{n+1} = 2x_n^2 - 1$, the invariant probability density function follows:
   $$f(x) = \frac{1}{\pi \sqrt{1 - x^2}}, \quad x \in (-1, 1)$$
2. **Empirical Discrepancy & Controlled Ablations:** The empirical KS distance is $D_X = 0.0312$ ($3.12\%$) and $D_Y = 0.0351$ ($3.51\%$). Controlled ablation experiments ($N=100,000$) demonstrate the physical mechanisms:
   - *Pure 1D Chebyshev Map:* $D = 0.0032$ ($p = 0.2424$, fails to reject ideal arcsine).
   - *Baseline ISC Map:* $D = 0.0298$ ($p = 1.44 \times 10^{-77}$).
   - *Ablation $d=0$ (constant rotation angle):* $D$ drops to $0.0108$ ($64\%$ reduction in deviation).
   - *Ablation $\omega=50$ (higher folding):* $D$ drops to $0.0077$.
   - *Ablation $\omega=100$ (high folding):* $D$ drops to $0.0017$ ($p = 0.9313$, converges to arcsine).
   *Finding:* The departure from ideal arcsine behavior is driven by nonlinear state-dependent rotation ($d/(1+x^2+y^2)$) and finite folding frequency ($\omega=10$, wrapping $X_n$ only $\approx 8$ times across $2\pi$).

- **Coordinate $y_n$:** Near-zero autocorrelation across all lags: $\rho_Y(1) = +0.009100$, maximum non-zero lag ACF $= +0.009100$.
- **Byte Sequences ($N = 1,000,000$):**

- Zero-lag Pearson linear correlation: $r(X, Y) = 0.005759$ ($p = 8.45 \times 10^{-9}$).
- Spearman rank correlation: $\rho_{\text{Spearman}}(X, Y) = 0.006109$ ($p = 9.99 \times 10^{-10}$).
- Peak cross-correlation: $\rho_{XY}(-1) = +0.135246$ at lag $k = -1$ (between $x_n$ and $y_{n-1}$).
- **Mutual Information Analysis ($256 \times 256$ Bins, $N=10^6$):**
  - Analytical Miller–Madow bias: $\text{Bias}_{\text{MM}} = (255 \times 255) / (2 \times 10^6 \times \ln 2) \approx 0.046906\text{ bits}$.

  - Circular time-shift mean (500 trials): $0.047849 \pm 0.000270\text{ bits}$.
  - Continuous states adjusted MI: $I_{\text{adj}}(X; Y) = 0.013765\text{ bits}$.
  - Quantised bytes adjusted MI ($k=14$): $I_{\text{adj}}(B_X; B_Y) = 0.000404\text{ bits} \approx 0.000000\text{ bits}$.
  - *Qualified Interpretation:* The observed plug-in mutual information is consistent with the finite-sample bias estimated using the Miller–Madow approximation and permutation-null trials. The current experiment provides no convincing evidence of substantial contemporaneous dependence between the quantised byte streams. It does not establish that the underlying variables are statistically independent.

### Section 8 — Key Findings

1. **Failure of Arccos Transformation:** Although Arccos achieves high Shannon entropy ($H \approx 7.996$ bits), its Chi-Square statistic exceeds $5,200$ ($p = 0.000$), decisively rejecting the uniform-byte null hypothesis. Crucially, Arccos preserves dynamical memory, exhibiting severe adjacent-byte autocorrelation ($\rho(1) \approx -0.0951$).
2. **Superiority of Scaled Modulo Transformation ($k \in \{13, 14, 15\}$):**
   - Achieves near-ideal Shannon entropy ($H > 7.9998$ bits).
   - Firmly passes Chi-Square goodness-of-fit testing ($p > 0.05$ across $k=13, 14, 15$).
   - Exhibits low linear autocorrelation ($|\rho| \le 0.0035$ across 100 lags on $N=10^6$).
   - Achieves $98.8\%$ to $99.0\%$ pass rates across $100$ independent sequences in multi-sequence NIST SP 800-22 testing.
3. **Exact Verification of the Float64 Precision Limit at $k=16$:**
   - Threshold $\theta = 2^{53} / 10^{16} = 0.9007199254740992$.
   - In the empirical trajectory, $281,822$ samples ($28.18\%$) for $X$ and $295,807$ samples ($29.58\%$) for $Y$ exceed $\theta$.
   - **$100.0\%$ of bytes generated in this region are strictly even (0 odd bytes generated).**
   - In the unaffected region ($|x_n| \le \theta$), the odd byte proportion is $49.92\%$.
   - This even-parity restriction causes the catastrophic $\chi^2 \approx 80,290$ failure, completely independent of 64-bit integer overflow.

### Section 9 — Limitations

1. **Finite Precision & Dynamical Degradation:** All experiments were conducted in IEEE 754 Float64. Over finite architectures, chaotic maps eventually collapse into periodic cycles.
2. **Empirical Entropy Limitations:** Shannon entropy measures marginal frequency uniformity only. It does not measure sequence unpredictability or Kolmogorov complexity.
3. **Statistical vs. Cryptographic Security:** Passing NIST SP 800-22 or Chi-Square tests demonstrates statistical pseudo-randomness, but does NOT establish resistance to known-plaintext, chosen-plaintext, or phase-space reconstruction attacks.

### Section 10 — Recommendation for ISC Research

**Technical Recommendation:**
The scaled-floor-modulo transformation with $k = 14$:
$$B_n = \lfloor |x_n| \times 10^{14} \rfloor \bmod 256$$
is a promising experimental byte-generation candidate under the tested configuration. Its observed marginal uniformity ($p = 0.822$ for $X$, $p = 0.975$ for $Y$), low linear autocorrelation ($|\rho| \le 0.0028$), and high multi-sequence NIST pass rates ($98.8\%$ to $99.0\%$) justify further investigation.

However, the transformation has **not** been established as a cryptographically secure pseudorandom generator or extractor. Subsequent encryption stages require separate cryptographic analysis, including evaluation against known-plaintext and chosen-plaintext attacks. Exponents $k \ge 16$ and the Arccos transformation are strictly rejected.
